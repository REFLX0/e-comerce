# Devis vs Site

## Short summary

The devis describes an MVP e-commerce website for oils and lubricants. The site in the repository is more complete than the proposal and already includes several advanced features that are not written in the devis.

## What the devis covers

- E-commerce storefront
- Product catalog
- Product pages
- Cart and checkout
- Simple admin area
- SEO basics
- Training and short support period
- Cash on delivery

## What the site already has

- Home page with featured products and category blocks
- Product catalogue with filters, sorting, pagination, and search
- Vehicle-based oil finder for compatible products
- Product detail pages with gallery, tabs, and related products
- Cart and checkout flow
- Customer account area with orders, wishlist, profile, addresses, security, and support
- Admin dashboard with orders, catalog, promotions, shipping, analytics, and settings
- Backend data model for vehicles, reviews, coupons, wishlist, and support tickets
- Docker and GitHub Actions deployment setup

## What is missing from the devis

- Vehicle compatibility and oil finder
- Wishlist
- Customer account features beyond a simple login or order form
- Support tickets and after-sales workflow
- Promotions and coupon management
- Reviews and review moderation
- Shipping management tools
- Analytics dashboard
- Advanced admin sections like inventory and settings
- Automated deployment and infrastructure details

## What is in the devis but not fully visible in the site

- The exact commercial packaging of the offer
- The timeline and milestone payment structure
- The guarantee of support and training as a business agreement
- The final legal and commercial document wording

## Main conclusion

The devis is valid as a basic launch proposal, but it underestimates the real scope of the website. If you want the proposal to match the site, it should mention the oil finder, customer account area, wishlist, coupons, reviews, support, shipping tools, analytics, and deployment setup.
